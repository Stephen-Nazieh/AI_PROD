AP Statistics — College Board Course Units (Exam Weighting)

Unit 1: Exploring One-Variable Data (15-23%) — categorical/quantitative data,
describing distributions (shape, center, spread), normal model, z-scores.
Unit 2: Exploring Two-Variable Data (5-7%) — scatterplots, correlation, least-
squares regression, residuals.
Unit 3: Collecting Data (12-15%) — sampling methods, bias, experiments vs.
observational studies, randomization.
Unit 4: Probability, Random Variables & Distributions (10-20%) — probability
rules, binomial and geometric distributions, expected value.
Unit 5: Sampling Distributions (7-12%) — the central limit theorem, sampling
distribution of a sample mean/proportion.
Unit 6: Inference for Categorical Data: Proportions (12-15%) — confidence
intervals and significance tests for one and two proportions.
Unit 7: Inference for Quantitative Data: Means (10-18%) — t-intervals and t-tests
for means, paired data.
Unit 8: Inference for Categorical Data: Chi-Square (2-5%) — goodness-of-fit,
independence, homogeneity.
Unit 9: Inference for Quantitative Data: Slopes (2-5%) — inference for the slope
of a regression line.

Exam: ~40% MCQ + free response. The big themes are distributions, sampling
distributions (the CLT bridge), and inference. Story arcs should build toward
inference as the payoff.
