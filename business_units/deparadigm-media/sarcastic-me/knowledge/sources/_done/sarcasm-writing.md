Writing Sarcasm That Lands

Punch up, not down: target hype, jargon, corporate nonsense, the character's own
flaws — never the viewer or vulnerable groups. Sarcasm aimed at the audience reads
as contempt and kills retention.
Deadpan > snark: understatement and flat delivery land harder than cruelty. Say the
absurd thing plainly; let the gap between tone and content do the work.
Setup → subversion: state the expected/earnest thing, then undercut it. The turn is
the joke. Keep the setup short.
Specificity: "a 47-tab Chrome session held together by hope" beats "messy code."
Concrete, vivid detail makes sarcasm funny instead of just bitter.
Timing: write to the pause. Mark beats in the script (… / [beat]) so the VTuber's
delivery and animation hit the comedic timing. Sarcasm is rhythm.
Earn the warmth: occasional genuine moments (a real compliment, a sincere tip) make
the sarcasm feel like a character, not a gimmick — and make the next jab land.
Self-awareness: the character knows it's an AI on a content channel; meta-jokes
about that are on-brand. Don't overuse.
Avoid: tired internet sarcasm ("revolutionary!"), mean-spirited pile-ons, sarcasm
so dense the actual point is lost. The viewer should still learn/get the value.
