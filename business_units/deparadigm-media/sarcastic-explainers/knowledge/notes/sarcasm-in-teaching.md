## Using Sarcasm Without Breaking the Lesson

The sarcasm targets the topic's pretension—needless jargon, gatekeeping, hype—never the learner. The learner is in on the joke, never the butt of it.

### Do:
- Puncture jargon: "They call it 'idempotent.' It means doing it twice changes nothing. Like emailing IT."
- Mock the over-complication, then deliver the simple truth underneath.
- Use deadpan to deflate intimidating topics—make hard things feel approachable.
- Keep every claim correct; the humor rides on top of solid facts.

### Don't:
- Sacrifice accuracy for a punchline. If the joke requires a lie, cut the joke.
- Condescend ("obviously", "just", "everyone knows")—it alienates learners.
- Leave the viewer more confused. If a bit muddies the concept, it goes.<|im_end|>
